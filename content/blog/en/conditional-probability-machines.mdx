“LLMs are just next-token predictors.”

Technically true. But I think it only tells half the story.

I prefer another name: **conditional probability machines**.

It sounds more technical, I know. But one word in there changes how you use these tools: _conditional_.

When a model writes its next word, it isn’t asking “what word usually comes next?”
It’s asking “what word comes next, given everything I’ve seen so far?”

In math, we write it like this:

```
P(next word | everything before it)
```

That little vertical bar means “given.” And it’s doing all the work.

“Everything before it” is much more than your last question:

- the instructions you typed
- hidden system instructions from the app
- the files and documents you uploaded
- the full chat history
- search results and tool outputs
- the words the model has already written in its own answer

![The same request, “Suggest a name.”, under two conditions. After “I just adopted a golden retriever puppy”, Max is the likeliest answer at 34%. After “We’re launching a cybersecurity startup”, Max falls to 1% and Sentinel leads at 29%. Illustrative probabilities.](/images/llm-conditional/en.png)

Look at the image. The request is the same: “Suggest a name.” Tell the model you just adopted a puppy, and “Max” is one of the most likely answers. Tell it you’re launching a cybersecurity startup, and “Max” almost disappears.

The model didn’t change. The condition did.

This holds for every LLM: Gemini, GPT, Claude, Llama, all of them. The model decides how it reacts to context. You decide what the context is.

It also explains three things we all run into:

**Vague prompts get generic answers.** Give the model little to condition on, and it falls back on the most average reply.

**Long chats drift.** Outdated or wrong information is still sitting in the context, still pulling on every prediction.

**One early mistake snowballs.** The model’s own words become part of the condition for everything that follows.

## What I do in practice

- Give the context a smart colleague would need: who it’s for, the goal, the constraints
- Paste the source material instead of hoping the model “knows” it
- When a chat gets messy, start fresh with a clean summary

Don’t just ask a better question. Build a better condition.

“Next-token prediction” describes what the model does.
“Conditional probability” describes what you control.
